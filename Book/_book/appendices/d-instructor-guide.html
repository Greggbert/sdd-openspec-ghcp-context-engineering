Appendix J — Instructor Guide

J.1 Course shape

Each morning is planned for 90 minutes and follows the same rhythm: establish one concept, demonstrate one artifact transition, let participants perform it, and review the resulting evidence.

Time Morning 1 Morning 2 Morning 3
0-15 min Context continuity and SDD Brownfield baseline Parallel-change risks
15-30 min OpenSpec structure and setup Additive vs. modified deltas Worktrees and isolation
30-55 min Explore and propose Add VIN search Propose and update filters
55-75 min Apply and test Modify unit-number length Apply, sync, and resolve overlap
75-90 min Archive and debrief Archive and compare specs Governance review and wrap-up

J.2 Teaching checkpoints

It may help to measure progress by more than whether the agent generated files. Pause at these points:

  1. After exploration, ask which ambiguities a person resolved.
  2. After proposal, invite participants to separate behavior from implementation.
  3. After apply, consider tracing one scenario through UI, API, persistence, and test code.
  4. After archive, read the main spec without referring to the completed delta.

J.3 Expected friction

Participants may type slash commands into the terminal, accept generated assumptions, or treat task checkmarks as test results. Keeping the terminal/chat distinction visible can help, as can asking for an artifact review before each apply or archive step.

Tool output may differ across OpenSpec releases or coding-agent models. Teach the invariant workflow and review criteria rather than requiring identical generated prose.

J.4 Recovery points

Maintain three instructor baselines:

  • Baseline A: Initialized project with no truck-search spec.
  • Baseline B: Archived unit-number search.
  • Baseline C: Archived VIN search and 10-character unit-number rule.

These baselines can keep a setup problem from consuming the next morning. They also let the instructor demonstrate a delta against a known source of truth.

J.5 Closing discussion

Ask participants to name one change in their own codebase where conflicting interpretations would cost more than writing a short spec. Then ask for one change too small or reversible to justify the workflow. The contrast can help keep SDD grounded as an engineering tradeoff rather than a universal ceremony.
