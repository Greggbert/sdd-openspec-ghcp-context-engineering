13  Modifying Accepted Behavior

The unit-number search from the earlier chapters has already passed review, reached both repositories, and entered the accepted OpenSpec specification. Now suppose a product owner asks for a smaller limit: “Could unit numbers have a maximum of 10 characters instead of requiring 15?”

That request may look like a one-line validation edit. Adding a second requirement, however, would leave two current rules for the same input: one requiring 15 characters and another allowing no more than 10. Developers, reviewers, and later Copilot sessions would have to guess which one wins.

OpenSpec treats this situation as a modification. The change would identify the accepted requirement, provide its complete replacement, and preserve every scenario that should remain part of that requirement. After archive, the main specification should describe one current rule, without asking readers to reconstruct precedence from change dates.

13.1 Learning objective

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to decide when a request modifies accepted behavior rather than adding a separate capability. You should also be able to write a complete MODIFIED requirement, trace its consequences through two repositories, and verify that archive would leave one coherent source of truth.

13.2 Recognize a replacement

Chapter 12 added VIN search without changing the identity of unit-number search. VIN introduced a new way to find a truck, so its requirement could sit beside the accepted unit-number requirement. The request in this chapter behaves differently. A unit number cannot remain both exactly 15 characters and at most 10 characters under the same conditions.

Before choosing a delta operation, complete this sentence:

After this change, the accepted requirement named Search by unit number should …

If the answer begins “still apply, plus users can do something separate,” ADDED may fit. If the answer begins “mean something different,” MODIFIED deserves closer attention. Removing the behavior entirely may call for REMOVED, while changing only its identity may call for RENAMED.

Question Likely operation
Will a separate requirement coexist with the accepted one? ADDED
Will the accepted requirement keep its identity but change meaning? MODIFIED
Will the accepted requirement cease to apply? REMOVED
Will its identity change while its intended behavior remains? RENAMED

Implementation files cannot settle this choice on their own. Two validators might share a helper even though they implement separate requirements, while one requirement might span an API, generated client, form, and several tests. The operation follows the relationship between accepted and proposed behavior, not the number or shape of files involved.

WarningA newer sentence does not cancel an older requirement

Appending “Unit numbers SHALL contain no more than 10 characters” under ADDED Requirements would leave the accepted 15-character rule in force. Neither recency nor proximity supplies replacement semantics. Use MODIFIED when review intends one requirement to supersede its accepted version.

13.3 Recover the accepted baseline

Before drafting a replacement, inspect the requirement as it exists under openspec/specs/. Record its heading exactly, along with every scenario nested beneath it. OpenSpec uses that requirement identity when applying the delta, and those scenarios reveal behavior that a short change request might otherwise erase.

For the lab, the baseline investigation might ask:

  • Does the requirement say exactly 15 characters, or no more than 15?
  • Does it describe trimming or another form of normalization?
  • Which cases cover matching, no match, empty input, and invalid input?
  • Do the backend and frontend specifications express the same boundary?
  • Which tests provide evidence for each accepted scenario?

A small baseline table can keep evidence separate from decisions:

Topic Accepted source Implementation evidence Decision needed?
Length rule Main unit-number specification Backend and frontend boundary tests Yes, replace 15 with the reviewed rule
Normalization Accepted scenarios, if present Controller, form, and tests Only if the request leaves it unclear
Empty input Accepted scenarios, if present Validation tests Only if the new meaning could differ
Match and no match Accepted scenarios Service, component, and browser tests Usually preserve unless scope says otherwise

If the specification, code, and tests disagree, record the mismatch as a review finding. Do not choose whichever behavior is easiest to edit. The team may resolve the mismatch inside this change when it affects the revised rule, or record separate work when it does not. Either choice should remain visible in the proposal.

13.4 Separate the request from its unanswered questions

“Maximum of 10 characters” answers less than it first appears to. Before proposal, the team still needs to decide:

  • whether values shorter than 10 characters qualify;
  • whether normalization occurs before the length check;
  • how the system handles empty and oversized values; and
  • whether case handling, matching, and no-match behavior change with the boundary.

An exploration prompt could make those gaps visible before proposal begins:

/opsx:explore

We are considering changing the accepted unit-number rule from 15 characters to a maximum of 10.

Please identify:
- the accepted requirement and all of its scenarios;
- every backend and frontend boundary that represents the current length rule;
- generated files and contracts affected by the rule;
- behaviors the request does not settle, including normalization and empty input; and
- tests that provide evidence for behavior likely to remain unchanged.

Do not modify files or infer product decisions from the current implementation.

Organize the resulting notes by what changes, what remains, and what still needs a decision:

Category Example Treatment in the change
Requested difference Replace the 15-character rule with a maximum of 10 State in the proposal and delta
Preserved behavior A valid value with no match keeps the accepted no-match outcome Restate its scenario in the replacement requirement
Open decision Whether trimming occurs before the length check Resolve before apply or mark as blocking

This separation adds a review step before coding. It also exposes disagreements before the backend and Angular repositories implement different interpretations.

13.5 Write the proposal around the behavioral change

Once review settles the open questions, the proposal can state both the intended difference and the preservation boundary. For this worked example, suppose the team chooses these exercise assumptions:

  • leading and trailing spaces are removed before validation;
  • one through 10 remaining characters are valid;
  • an empty value remains invalid;
  • 11 or more remaining characters are invalid;
  • matching remains exact; and
  • a valid value with no match returns no matching trucks.

Those are example product decisions, not consequences of the word MODIFIED. Another team might choose different answers and still use the same OpenSpec operation.

A compact proposal could read:

# Change: Reduce the unit-number length limit

## Why

The business now assigns unit numbers containing between one and 10 characters.  The search still
requires exact matching after removing leading and trailing spaces.

## What Changes

- Remove leading and trailing spaces before validating the unit number.
- Accept between one and 10 remaining characters.
- Reject empty values and values longer than 10 characters.
- Preserve exact matching and the existing no-match result.
- Publish the revised constraint through the backend-owned OpenAPI contract.

## Non-Goals

- Changes to VIN search
- Partial or fuzzy unit-number matching
- New result fields or response states

Naming unchanged behavior limits the change. A length edit can invite nearby cleanup or a new matching strategy, but the non-goals and preservation claims give reviewers a boundary for those edits without copying unrelated specifications into the delta.

13.6 Replace the complete requirement

A MODIFIED section should contain the whole requirement as it ought to read after archive. It should not contain a patch fragment such as “change 15 to 10,” because that fragment would depend on a reader finding and mentally merging the accepted version.

Using the exercise assumptions, a backend delta might look like this:

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: Search by unit number

The API SHALL remove leading and trailing spaces from a unit-number search value.  The remaining
value SHALL contain between one and 10 characters, and the API SHALL match it exactly against unit
numbers.

#### Scenario: Matching unit number is at the maximum length

- **GIVEN** a truck has unit number `TRUCK-1234`
- **WHEN** a client searches for `TRUCK-1234`
- **THEN** the API returns that truck

#### Scenario: Matching unit number is shorter than the maximum

- **GIVEN** a truck has unit number `TRK-123`
- **WHEN** a client searches for `TRK-123`
- **THEN** the API returns that truck

#### Scenario: Matching unit number has surrounding spaces

- **GIVEN** a truck has unit number `TRUCK-1234`
- **WHEN** a client searches for `  TRUCK-1234  `
- **THEN** the API returns that truck

#### Scenario: Unit number exceeds the maximum

- **WHEN** a client searches for `TRUCK-12345`
- **THEN** the API rejects the search value as invalid

#### Scenario: Unit number is empty

- **WHEN** a client searches with a value that is empty after removing surrounding spaces
- **THEN** the API rejects the search value as invalid

#### Scenario: No truck matches

- **GIVEN** no truck has unit number `TRUCK-9999`
- **WHEN** a client searches for `TRUCK-9999`
- **THEN** the API returns no matching trucks

This example is complete only under the stated exercise assumptions. Compare it with the actual lab requirement before using it. If that requirement contains another match, failure, or response scenario, carry the scenario forward unless the proposal deliberately changes or removes it.

The requirement heading deserves special care. If the accepted heading reads Search by unit number, changing it casually to Validate a unit number may prevent a clean identity match or misrepresent the requirement’s scope. Copy the accepted heading, then revise the body and scenarios beneath it.

NoteComplete does not mean duplicated

Completeness applies to the requirement being replaced. The delta need not copy every requirement in the capability, nor should it repeat unrelated VIN behavior. Include the revised requirement and all scenarios that should survive within that requirement.

13.7 Inspect the delta before implementation

OpenSpec validation can catch malformed delta structure, while a behavioral diff can expose an accidental deletion or an unchanged 15-character clause. Run the repository’s configured checks, then inspect the proposed difference:

openspec validate reduce-unit-number-limit --strict
openspec show reduce-unit-number-limit --diff

Review the diff against these checks:

  • Match the modified heading to the accepted requirement.
  • Confirm that archive would replace the 15-character wording rather than retain it beside the new rule.
  • Account for every scenario named by the proposal.
  • Remove implementation choices, such as Java annotations or Angular validators, from the behavioral specification.
  • Confirm that each boundary scenario could fail for the intended reason before implementation.

A passing structural check cannot answer all of those questions. Human review still needs to judge whether the replacement captures the business decision and whether its preservation claims are credible.

13.8 Trace one rule through two repositories

The modified requirement may be small, but the old constraint may have crossed several ownership boundaries. Search for the old value and for the concepts that express it. A literal search for 15 could find noise and miss a named constant, a schema facet, or prose that says “fifteen,” so combine value searches with searches for requirement names, validation messages, and test cases.

Boundary What may need review What would count as drift?
Backend request validation Length annotation, validator, or request parsing API still accepts 11 through 15 characters
Search behavior Service and persistence tests Matching changes even though the proposal preserves it
OpenAPI document maxLength, descriptions, and responses Published schema still advertises 15
Generated Angular client Regenerated request types or metadata Hand edit disappears on regeneration
Angular form Validators, feedback, and submission state UI rejects a valid 10-character value or accepts 11
Visible text Labels, hints, and errors User sees the obsolete limit
Automated tests Boundary and preserved-behavior cases Tests prove only the new happy path

The backend should continue to own the wire contract. After its validation and OpenAPI output reflect the reviewed delta, inspect the contract diff before regenerating the Angular client. Form tests and UI changes may proceed against the reviewed frontend delta, but client integration should wait for the backend-owned contract if generation is the established handoff.

Avoid editing generated client files as though they owned the rule. A manual fix could make the current branch compile, yet regeneration would restore whatever the OpenAPI document still says. The durable correction belongs in the source contract and generation workflow.

13.9 Apply in an order that preserves evidence

A task list might keep the dependency visible without prescribing every class or function:

  1. Add or revise backend tests for 10-character, shorter valid, 11-character, and empty values.
  2. Update backend validation while preserving accepted match, no-match, and error behavior.
  3. Generate and review the OpenAPI document for the revised constraint and unrelated drift.
  4. Regenerate the Angular client from the reviewed contract.
  5. Update Angular validation, visible feedback, and focused component tests.
  6. Exercise the integrated path and rerun preserved-behavior tests in both repositories.

Begin with boundary evidence. A test for exactly 10 characters and another for 11 can show whether the implementation changed the intended edge rather than merely replacing one constant. Tests for matching and no match then check the proposal’s claim that the rest of the search behavior survived.

During apply, ask Copilot to work from scenario names and report the evidence for each one. A message such as “updated max length everywhere” would reveal little about normalization, generated artifacts, or regression coverage. A scenario-to-test trace gives the reviewer something more specific to inspect.

13.10 Verify the replacement before archive

Verification has two jobs: show that the implementation adopted the new boundary and show that the change preserved everything it claimed not to alter.

Claim Focused evidence
A normalized 10-character value is accepted Backend boundary test and Angular form test
A normalized value shorter than 10 is accepted Backend and frontend validation tests
An 11-character value is rejected API validation test and visible-feedback test
Empty input follows the reviewed rule Backend and Angular empty-input tests
Match and no-match behavior remain intact Existing service, component, and integrated tests
Both repositories share the revised contract Reviewed OpenAPI diff and generated-client diff
The old rule no longer survives Search across specs, code, generated output, tests, and UI text

Run focused checks first, then the broader repository suites and integrated path. If an 11-character value fails in the UI but reaches the backend through another path, the replacement has not reached every boundary. If all tests pass while the generated schema still reports 15, the repositories still disagree about their contract.

Before archive, inspect the source-of-truth result that synchronization would produce. Confirm that the main specification would contain one complete unit-number requirement with the 10-character maximum and every preserved scenario. If understanding the current rule still requires an older archived change, the active delta remains incomplete.

After archive, repeat the obsolete-assumption search against the main specification and generated artifacts. The dated change record may retain the old rule as history; the active source of truth should not present it as current behavior.

13.11 Hands-on exercise

Use the two lab repositories to propose, apply, verify, and archive the revised unit-number rule. Keep a compact record that another reviewer could follow without replaying the Copilot chat:

Product change: reduce-unit-number-limit
Accepted requirement: Search by unit number
Old rule: <exact accepted wording>
Reviewed replacement: <exact revised wording>
Preserved scenarios: <scenario names>
Open questions: <owners and blocking status>
Backend evidence: <commands and results>
Contract evidence: <OpenAPI and generated-client diffs>
Frontend evidence: <commands and results>
Post-archive search: <obsolete assumptions found or none>

Pause when exploration exposes an unanswered product decision. Revise the delta after that decision receives review, then resume implementation from the updated scenarios. A code-only choice or a corrective chat message would leave the next agent with the same ambiguity.

13.12 Verification checkpoint

The change may be ready to close when the modified heading matches the accepted requirement, the delta supplies its complete replacement, and strict validation passes. Focused tests should cover both sides of the new boundary, while regression evidence should cover the scenarios promised as unchanged. The OpenAPI and generated-client diffs should describe the same maximum, and a search should find no current 15-character assumption outside historical records.

If one of those checks fails, keep the change open. Update the artifact that owns the discrepancy, repeat the narrow check, and inspect the would-be source of truth again before archive.

13.13 What you learned

MODIFIED means supplying the exact requirement that should exist after archive, not editing only the sentence that changed. Archive can preserve only the scenarios present in that replacement, so an omitted scenario changes the source of truth whether the omission was deliberate or not.
